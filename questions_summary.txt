Case Scenario:1
Ramu's Tea Stall at IIT Madras
Ramu’s tea stall was one of the most famous hotspots at IIT Madras, located near the Himalaya
Mess on the campus. While he served a variety of snacks, cold drinks, and other refreshments, his
tea, coffee, and samosas were the most popular items, consistently generating the highest sales. On
any given day, Ramu operates with a fixed set of resources: a specific stall size, two burners, a
limited amount of labour hours, and fixed daily supplies of milk, tea leaves, and coffee powder.
With these resources, his primary focus is producing two major staple items: Tea and Filter Coffee.
The PPF is a curve that shows the maximum possible output combinations of two goods an
economy (or a business like Ramu's) can produce using all available resources efficiently.
Here is how Ramu’s daily production frontier looks:
Ramu is currently operating efficiently on his PPF, producing 300 cups of tea and 150 cups of filter
coffee daily. During a major campus festival, demand for filter coffee spikes. If Ramu decides to
produce 200 cups of filter coffee, what must happen to his tea production?
   A. It will increase because higher coffee sales generate more revenue to buy tea leaves.
   B. It will remain at 300 cups because making coffee uses different ingredients than making tea. According to the Production Possibility Frontier (PPF), a trade-off exists between the two items: reducing the production of Coffee allows for an increase in Tea production, and vice versa. For Ramu to achieve operating efficiency, he must produce at a point directly on the PPF curve.
   C. It will drop to zero because a stall can only specialize in one efficient product at a time.
   D. It will decrease because resources must be shifted from tea to coffee production. [*CORRECT*]
Feedback: According to the Production Possibility Frontier (PPF), a trade-off exists between the two items: reducing the production of Coffee allows for an increase in Tea production, and vice versa. For Ramu to achieve operating efficiency, he must produce at a point directly on the PPF curve.
